# Proposal BAB I–III

Use for outline, drafting and proposal review. Build the argument before filling chapters: What is investigated → why it matters → what evidence/literature establishes → what remains uncertain → RQ/objective → how evidence can answer it. Use the relevant reasoning/design routes for missing links.

## Guideline-first writing contract

Read supplied campus/program requirements, mark their governed mandatory structure and map research state into it. Without one, label BAB I problem/introduction, BAB II theoretical/empirical foundation and BAB III methodology as provisional conceptual organization; subchapters are not universal.

For each section specify purpose, key questions, evidence needed, dependencies, expected content, forbidden shortcuts and completion criteria. For background, connect context, phenomenon/evidence, problem and consequences, existing knowledge, unresolved issue and research direction. Avoid irrelevant history, unsupported statistics, invented references or jumping to a solution without a problem.

BAB II should synthesize source evidence, define relevant concepts/theory and justify the framework/RQ/design; a list of summaries is insufficient. BAB III explains how design, data, participants/sampling, instruments/collection, analysis, ethics and feasibility answer each RQ. Planned actions stay future/planned; do not invent results to satisfy an IMRaD pattern.

## Drafting gate

- READY: evidence and major decisions suffice for the requested section.
- PARTIALLY_READY: draft supported parts and visibly mark missing evidence/decisions, with exact next action.
- NOT_READY: a material missing link prevents meaningful drafting; offer an argument outline and evidence plan rather than generic paragraphs presented as a completed chapter.

These are section-readiness outcomes. The researcher chooses material scope/design; readiness is not supervisor approval. Use [title/proposal helpers](../assets/title-proposal-templates.md) only for the requested artifact.

## Supervisor expectations and self-assessment

Record explicit feedback, rubric, written guidance or actual accepted decisions with locator and scope. Distinguish those from an inference about preference; never say a lecturer 'always wants' something without evidence. Reconcile conflicts with campus guidance visibly.

Review foundation, practical problem/gap evidence, question quality/answerability, literature/theory, design/data/sampling, analysis, ethics/feasibility, BAB I–III consistency, guideline fit and open items. Report readiness by affected section and concrete unresolved dependency, without a cosmetic score or ACC guarantee. A changed research question triggers method/data and outline review, not just paragraph editing.
