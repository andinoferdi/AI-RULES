# Research-design routing

Use RQ/objective, needed evidence, data properties, intended inference, domain/guideline, access and approved decisions to select a route. A keyword or the perceived prestige of a method is insufficient. Preserve a locked research type and surface material conflicts rather than silently replacing it.

## Quantitative

Use when measurement, distributions, comparisons, association, estimation or a justified causal/evaluation question fits the evidence need. Determine whether intervention/assignment exists and what inference is supportable before selecting tests. Include constructs, operationalization, population/frame/sample, measurement and assumptions only where relevant. A descriptive study need not have X/Y or hypotheses. A pre/post (before/after) comparison alone does not establish an experimental design, random assignment, control or causal identification without explicit assignment, comparator and counterfactual reasoning. Load quantitative planning for the deeper design-to-analysis chain.

## Qualitative

For experience, meaning, process or contextual understanding, connect phenomenon/focus to a justified design and participants/informants or documentary sources. Explain participant selection and access, interview/observation/document collection, coding/categorization and suitable thematic/content or other justified analysis. Keep an audit trail from source to code/theme/interpretation; consider reflexivity, credibility and trustworthiness in ways appropriate to the design. Saturation is applicable reasoning, not a universal magic participant count. Do not force statistical hypotheses, independent/dependent variables or probability-population framing. No fabricated interview quotes or already-conducted fieldwork.

## Mixed method

State why either strand alone is insufficient, which question each answers, strand priority/timing, how sampling/data connect and the actual integration point. Convergent designs bring parallel strands into comparison/integration; explanatory sequential designs use a later qualitative strand to help explain quantitative results; exploratory sequential designs use qualitative work to inform subsequent quantitative development/examination. Select logic from need, not a compulsory typology. Plan a joint display or explicit linking/integration procedure and how agreement/divergence will inform combined interpretation. A questionnaire plus interviews without integration is not enough.

## R&D

Connect evidenced need → intended product/intervention/model → stakeholders/requirements → justified development model → prototype/development → appropriate validation/testing/evaluation → revision → final artifact and bounded contribution. Developing software, applications or instructional media is primarily an R&D or design-science lifecycle, not automatically a quantitative experiment. Do not classify development work as an experiment merely because user testing or evaluation occurs; add an experimental or quasi-experimental strand only when an explicit intervention, comparator/control and causal hypothesis are justified. Select ADDIE, 4D, Borg & Gall, Design Science or another model only with domain/guideline/need rationale; verify exact source-dependent procedures before attributing them. Distinguish feasibility/usability, expert appraisal and effectiveness evidence; an operational product does not prove learning effectiveness.

## Sampling and participant decisions

Identify unit, population or participant context, required inference, frame/access, inclusion/exclusion and ethics before strategy. Probability sampling supports particular design-based inferences only when actually implemented; convenience access does not prove representativeness. Non-probability/purposive participant selection needs fit-to-question rationale and explicit generalization limits. Sample-size rationale depends on design, target precision/power where applicable, information needs, resources and access, not a universal number or inherited habit.

Return chosen/provisional route, evidence chain, alternatives where material, inference limits and unresolved dependencies. If data cannot answer the RQ, offer a feasible design/question adaptation for the researcher to decide.
