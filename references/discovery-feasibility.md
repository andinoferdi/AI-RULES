# Discovery and feasibility

Use for an undeveloped interest, phenomenon or research direction. Enter the existing state rather than reopening an approved topic.

Build a flexible reasoning map: area/theme → phenomenon → observed evidence → practical problem or knowledge question → possible research direction → necessary data/design → contribution → provisional title. Distinguish an observed condition from a stakeholder expectation or assumption. Links should explain why the next element follows; a decorative mind-map with unrelated keywords is insufficient.

For a raw interest such as AI in education, identify a tractable context, the phenomenon the user can observe, the question they want answered and realistic data access. Ask only the missing decision that changes the direction. You can offer a few clearly provisional directions with their required evidence; do not invent declining achievement statistics or settled research gaps.

Assess problem clarity, relevance, data availability, access to subjects/objects, participant/sample practicality, collection and analysis feasibility, time, tools, expertise, ethics/privacy, organizational dependencies, scope and intended contribution. Use actual constraints; unknown permission or access is not a favorable score.

Return FEASIBLE, FEASIBLE_WITH_RISK, NEEDS_CLARIFICATION, BLOCKED or NOT_YET_ASSESSABLE with the basis, affected option and next evidence/action. These are feasibility outcomes, not field statuses. Avoid numerical rankings without a justified rubric. A feasible study still needs a supported problem and design; feasibility does not prove novelty or approval.

Differentiate options by evidence needs: interview access for an experience question; appropriate measurement/frame for estimating patterns; integrated strands for mixed research; stakeholder need and evaluable artifact for R&D. Preserve the user's choice while making tradeoffs visible. A blocked data source can justify proposing a narrower scope or alternative source; changing the main research direction remains the researcher's decision.
