# Problem, gap, contribution and research questions

Use when developing or checking the argument behind a study. A practical difficulty is not automatically a knowledge gap.

Start with actual condition and its evidence. If comparison to an expected condition helps, identify whether that expectation is stakeholder preference, regulation, theory or an unsupported assumption. Explain the consequence and the researchable uncertainty; do not manufacture a problem from a desired solution.

For a gap, compare what relevant literature establishes with what remains insufficiently explained, tested, compared or resolved. Record supporting sources, search/reading coverage and limitations. A new location, different keyword or divergent study result alone does not establish novelty. Mark insufficient coverage NEEDS_EVIDENCE and identify the next search/comparison instead of asserting nobody has studied it.

For contribution, state prior knowledge, the proposed material addition/refinement/test, who it matters to, its realistic scope and comparative evidence. Empirical, contextual, methodological, data, framework, integration or evaluation contributions may fit; choose from the study's actual purpose. Do not inflate contribution to match an academic-level stereotype.

Gap spotting identifies missing knowledge within an existing framing. Problematization identifies a specific underlying assumption, evidence it may be inadequate, and what alternative framing would change. It is not merely renaming a gap. Use it only when the literature supports the challenged assumption and the study can examine it. State-of-the-art claims require cross-paper synthesis, not a list of abstracts.

Check each RQ for clarity, focus, answerability with realistically obtainable evidence, problem/objective alignment, method fit, concept fit, feasible access/time/ethics and absence of an embedded conclusion. For each question state the required evidence and what analysis would answer it. If an experience question is paired only with a numerical association test, address the design mismatch rather than polishing the sentence.

Keep focus, context/location, time period, unit/population, process/system, concept and data boundaries explicit when material. 'Lotus' and exact proprietary gap taxonomies have no supplied definitions here: ask for the authorized definition when needed or use plain boundary reasoning; do not invent their meaning.
