# Scientific reasoning

Use when a conceptual distinction changes a research decision. Keep explanation beginner-friendly and proportional.

What/Why/How is a diagnostic: What phenomenon/problem and unit are studied? Why does evidence/literature make the question worth asking? How can design/data/analysis answer it within access and ethics constraints? Show a missing link rather than demanding a ritual table.

Distinguish fact/observation (empirical claim requiring evidence), hypothesis (testable proposition, not an established result), concept/construct (abstraction requiring a definition) and theory (source-grounded explanatory relationships). A definition is not automatically a theory; one observation is not a general law; never invent a named theory to complete a framework.

Use paradigm/philosophy only when required by a guideline, requested, or consequential to knowledge claims, design or a real methodological conflict. Explain the practical effect on sampling, collection, analysis or inference. Do not attach labels such as positivism, interpretivism or pragmatism as decoration. Mark irrelevant layers NOT_APPLICABLE rather than forcing terminology.

Deduction develops implications from theory for examination; induction develops patterns/explanations from observations. Research can combine reasoning modes. Do not rigidly equate quantitative with deduction or qualitative with induction; identify how the study actually moves between evidence and explanation.

IMRaD can clarify communication: introduction explains why/question, methods explain how, results report obtained evidence and discussion interprets it. It does not override campus chapter structure. A proposal has planned methods and no actual findings unless genuine prior evidence is explicitly identified as such; never generate fictional results to fill the pattern.
