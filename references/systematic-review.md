# Systematic-review gate and reporting

Use only when the research question requires a systematic review or the user explicitly selects it. Distinguish planning a systematic review from having executed one. Narrative/thesis review can support theory/gap without claiming exhaustive retrieval. Scoping/rapid/other review labels require their chosen method and an appropriate source, not a more impressive name.

## Planning and execution gate

Before execution, establish question/type, protocol, eligibility, justified information sources/coverage, reproducible queries and dates, deduplication, screening procedure, extraction fields, appropriate quality/risk appraisal, synthesis plan, applicable registration requirements and reporting guidance. Identify reviewers/roles and how disagreements will be resolved where the method requires it. Unmet material prerequisites produce a planning artifact and named blocker; do not claim systematic execution.

Before reporting a completed review, require actual search exports/logs, record/report/study registry, screening decisions and reportable full-text exclusions, extractions, appraisal where relevant and executed synthesis evidence. Many keywords or a large paper count is insufficient. An unavailable database is an explicit coverage limitation; do not invent an executed search to fill it.

Use the search and reading routes for their mechanics without duplicating them. Preserve protocol versions and amendments, including post-hoc changes and reasons. Extract study identity, context/sample, design, intervention/exposure/concepts, outcomes/findings/uncertainty, limitations and provenance suitable for the question. Keep reviewer judgments distinct from source-reported facts. Use design-appropriate appraisal rather than journal prestige as a quality score. Synthesis/meta-analysis requires compatible evidence and justified method; do not pool merely because numbers exist.

## Count integrity and reporting

Derive flow counts from actual registry operations. Retain identifiers linking duplicates and reports to studies; distinguish records identified, removed before screening, screened/excluded, reports sought/not retrieved, full texts assessed/excluded with reasons, included reports and included studies. Check conservation at each relevant branch with actual categories; reports and studies need not be equal. Unknown counts stay unknown, never plausible filler.

[PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020) is reporting guidance with checklists and flow diagrams; it is not a protocol, analysis method or quality certificate. Select relevant extensions for the review type. [PRISMA-S](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-search) supports transparent search reporting: preserve databases/platforms, exact strategies, dates, restrictions, supplementary searches and record handling. A completed checklist does not establish that the underlying actions occurred.

Gate the final claim by coverage, auditability, extraction/appraisal and synthesis evidence; report methodological/access limitations and unresolved corrections/retractions. If prerequisites remain missing, call the output a systematic-review plan or the actual supported review type, not a completed systematic review. AI may assist screening/extraction but must not conceal human review requirements, uncertainty or unverified source content.
