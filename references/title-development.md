# Structured title development

Use to create, compare or revise titles from current research decisions. When the user supplies only a raw, broad topic without problem context, focus or access, do not immediately generate a list of arbitrary title candidates. Route to [Discovery and feasibility](discovery-feasibility.md) first: identify known and unknown context, offer direction questions or plain research goals to narrow the focus, and ask the minimal context or access question needed before formulating titles.

Otherwise state one sentence: 'This research aims to …'. If focus/question/scope/design are inadequate, mark title readiness NOT_READY or PARTIALLY_READY and identify the missing decision. Only use READY when the basis is sufficiently supported; provisional candidates must remain labeled.

Trace problem → core focus → RQ/objective → object/subject/context → relevant variable, factor, concept or construct → design → scope → wording. Do not add concepts just to make a title sound academic. Qualitative experience studies need not have X/Y; R&D may foreground need, product, user and evaluation; mixed titles should express the integrated purpose.

For each candidate identify only relevant anatomy: core focus, relationship/action, variable/factor or concept/construct, object/subject, population, context/location/time, method/design, intended outcome and scope qualifier. Apply an explicit inclusion decision before adding method, location or time: explain whether each component changes substantive scope/meaning or is required by the institution. If only 'looks complete' or 'sounds academic' motivates it, retain the focused title without it and explain why omission is preferred. Offer an explicitly labeled optional variant only after a defensible substantive reason exists.

Offer proportionate candidates, normally a few when no count is requested. Distinguish a substantive alternative (focus, unit, scope, evaluative emphasis) from a style variant of the same research. When title styles are requested, produce usable, actual title formulations in Indonesian or target academic convention, not study-description sentences ('Penelitian ini bertujuan…'). Explain framing differences while preserving RQ, design and claim:

- Interrogative: frame the supported research question as a usable question title when disciplinary convention permits.
- Descriptive: name what is described, understood, mapped, compared or evaluated.
- Declarative: state a bounded action or structural relationship without asserting an unverified result, finding or causation the design cannot support; if an asserted finding is unverified, use an action/design formulation or explain why the claim cannot yet be stated.

Check causal words such as 'meningkatkan', 'memengaruhi', 'menyebabkan' and 'efektif terhadap' against actual design/inference. Reframe unsupported causation as association, description or evaluation as appropriate. A proposed intervention is not an already demonstrated improvement.

Before recommending, check clarity, problem/RQ/objective alignment, scope, concept fit, method fit, necessity of context, feasibility, traceability, readability and institutional fit. Explain tradeoffs and remaining evidence, not arbitrary quality scores. Rationale comes from existing state: problem, represented question, selected concept, object/context, method if shown, intended contribution and scope.

Never guarantee ACC, supervisor preference or no future revisions. For rejection, inspect actual feedback and determine whether it concerns focus, scope, concept, method, contribution, convention or wording. Revise the underlying state first if necessary, propagate material changes and then formulate the title. If only wording is requested, preserve the underlying study.
