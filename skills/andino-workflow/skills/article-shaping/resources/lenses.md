# Lenses for moves, openings and article shapes

Internal resource for `andino-article-shaping`. Reach for a lens when your candidates come
out alike and you need a different way into the same pile. A lens points you at material;
it never supplies material. Every candidate is still built from the pile, as step 4 of the
procedure requires, and the author never sees these names.

## Block moves

Each block makes one move. Naming the move to yourself helps you see whether two candidates
really differ.

| Move | What it does for the reader | Reach for it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scene | Puts the reader in one concrete moment from the pile | Opening, or re-anchoring after a stretch of explanation |
| Claim | States a point the pile defends | The reader has what they need to accept it |
| Question | Opens a gap the next blocks will close | The pile holds a real contradiction or open question |
| Aside | Adds a candid limit, doubt or boundary | The author's notes admit uncertainty, or a reader could misapply the point |
| Turn | Changes direction: the obvious fix fails, the cause was elsewhere | The pile records a reversal or a rejected option |
| Payoff | Delivers what the opening promised | The promise can now be kept with what the page holds |

## Opening angles

Each angle names the pile material it needs. When the pile lacks it, the angle is not
available for this article; never supply the material yourself.

- **Start in the moment.** Needs a specific incident the pile describes: what happened,
  where, who noticed. Use only the details the pile gives.
- **Start on the claim.** Needs a point the pile argues, ideally in the author's own words.
  The rest of the article earns it.
- **Start on the contradiction.** Needs two facts in the pile that pull against each other.
  The article resolves them.
- **Start on the image.** Needs a metaphor or comparison the author wrote. Works when the
  image carries the thesis; it lands weaker before the reader knows what happened.

Different angles usually imply different theses. If two candidates share a thesis, change
the angle, not the wording.

## Article shapes

A shape is a rough order of moves. Use one to check whether the next candidates move the
article forward; drop it the moment the pile pulls elsewhere.

- **Narrative arc:** situation, rising trouble, the turn, resolution. Fits incident write-ups
  and personal stories.
- **Insight first:** the point, how it works, edges and limits, what to do. Fits guides and
  decisions.
- **Problem to resolution:** how things were, the hidden crack, why it got worse, what
  changed. Fits arguments for a new practice.
- **Small detail to principle:** one ordinary observation, the pattern behind it, the rule
  it suggests. Fits reflective essays.
