# Format and prose

Internal resource for `andino-article-shaping`. Load it when choosing how a block is
rendered, or when checking a drafted block before you show it.

## Argue the format out loud

Choose each block's container deliberately and say why in one line when more than prose is
plausible.

| Choice | Choose | Avoid | Rationalisation to reject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prose or list | Prose to carry an argument or a chain of causes; a list for items that are truly parallel | Turning a causal argument into bullets | "Lists are easier to scan." Bullets cut the links between dependent steps. |
| Inline or callout | A callout (`> [!NOTE]`, `> [!TIP]`) for a tip or warning that would derail the argument inline | A callout every few paragraphs | "Callouts look modern." Too many make an essay read like reference docs. |
| Table or repeated structure | A table when three or more items share the same fields | Tables for uneven comparisons or commentary | "Tables summarise neatly." A premature table kills the voice. |
| Quote or paraphrase | A direct quote when the original wording is the point | Long block quotes where three words carry the point | "Quotes add authority." Unneeded quotes slow the reader. |
| Code block or inline code | A block for multi-line, runnable or illustrative code; inline for one identifier | Dumping boilerplate around the lines that matter | "Show the full script." Show the load-bearing lines. |

## Prose the author would sign

The article is written in the author's language and register, taken from the pile: casual
notes produce a casual engineering voice, formal notes a formal one. Before showing a
candidate, reread it and rewrite anything below in that register.

| Pattern | English example | Indonesian example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throat-clearing opener | "In today's fast-paced world..." | "Di era digital yang serba cepat ini..." |
| Contrast nobody asked for | "It's not about X, it's about Y" when no one said X | "Bukan X, tapi Y" when no one said X; the author's own quote is exempt |
| Triad built for rhythm | "fast, reliable and scalable" | "cepat, andal, dan efisien" |
| Stacked dashes | Several em dashes in one paragraph | The same |
| Recap closer | A last line that restates the paragraph | "Pada akhirnya..." |
| Clipped fragments for drama | "It broke. Again. Badly." | "Rusak. Lagi. Parah." |
| Inflated words | leverage, delve, seamless, robust, pivotal | "tak dapat dipungkiri", "memegang peranan penting" |
| Translated phrasing | Word order carried over from another language | "Hal ini dikarenakan oleh..." |

Vary sentence length because the content varies, not by count. Keep the author's images,
idioms and plain terms; a vivid phrase in the pile beats a polished paraphrase.

Sentence-level editing of finished prose is `natural-writing`'s job. Your messages to the
author follow Focus when it is active; the article follows the author.
