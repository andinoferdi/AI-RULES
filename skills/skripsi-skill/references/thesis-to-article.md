# Thesis to journal article

Load when a student wants to turn a skripsi, tesis or disertasi into a journal article,
for example for a SINTA-accredited journal. The target journal's template and author
guidelines override everything here. Venue checks stay in
[advanced methodology](advanced-methodology-integrity.md) (due diligence) and current
accreditation facts in [Indonesian conventions](indonesian-academic-conventions.md).

## Choose the article

| Strategy | When it fits | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One article from the core research question | Most skripsi; one clear finding | Keep one argument; drop side questions |
| Results-focused article | Several findings, one strong enough alone | Do not split one dataset into thin articles |
| Method or instrument article | The thesis developed or validated an instrument or product | Needs validation evidence, not only the product |
| Review article | Only when the thesis contains a documented review method | A literature chapter is not a systematic review |

Ask which journal is targeted before shaping the draft; its scope, length limit,
structure and citation style decide the rest. Without a target, use a generic structure
and mark journal-specific parts `[PERLU KEPUTUSAN: jurnal sasaran]`.

## Map chapters to sections

| Thesis | Article | Compression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BAB I | Pendahuluan | Problem, compared evidence, gap and aim in a few paragraphs |
| BAB II | Inside Pendahuluan or a short Kajian Pustaka | Keep only literature the argument and discussion use |
| BAB III | Metode | Design, participants or data, instruments, analysis; enough to replicate |
| BAB IV | Hasil dan Pembahasan | Main findings with key tables; discussion per finding |
| BAB V | Simpulan | Direct answer to the aim; brief limitations and implication |

## Compression rules

- Keep one argument from introduction to conclusion; remove material that does not serve it.
- Cut textbook definitions, long theory histories and institutional background.
- Keep every number traceable to the thesis output; never round or change values to fit.
- Rewrite rather than paste: journal prose is denser than thesis prose.
- Tables: keep those the argument needs; merge or move the rest to the thesis.
- References: keep cited works only, verified ([citation verification](citation-verification.md)).

## Abstract and keywords

Write the abstract last, in the journal's required languages, each written separately:
problem and aim, method, main result with key numbers, conclusion. Choose keywords the
journal's readers would search, not words already in the title only.

## Authorship and publication ethics

- Authorship, author order and whether supervisors are co-authors are decided by the
  student and supervisors under campus and journal rules; never assume them.
- Check the journal's rules on prior publication of the thesis in a repository and on
  overlap with the thesis text; disclose as required.
- Follow the campus and journal rules on AI-use disclosure.
- Verify the journal before submitting: current accreditation on the official portal,
  scope, fees, review process and warning signs of predatory practice.

## Checklist

- Target journal identified, or its absence marked.
- One argument, aligned title, aim, method, results and conclusion.
- All numbers and quotes traceable; references verified and cited.
- Length and structure follow the journal; abstract written last in each language.
- Authorship and disclosure decided by the people responsible, not by the assistant.
